Address by the CEA (UK) President, Professor Shujie Yao (University of Portsmouth)
April 1999, CEA 10th Annual Conference, Middlesex University, London
Your Excellency Ambassador, distinguished guests, members of the CEA, ladies and gentlemen: I am extremely honoured to be a part of this important occasion marking the opening of the 10th Chinese Economic Association Annual Conference. On behalf of the CEA (UK), I would like to extend a warm welcome to all our esteemed guests, especially those who have travelled from afar to join us at this occasion.
The CEA was founded in 1988 and its aim is to advance the knowledge of the general British public about economic development in China and to promote research on the Chinese economy. Thanks to the persistent efforts of our members over the last 10 years, the CEA has now become an established academic and cultural centre for promoting studies on the Chinese economy in the UK. The most important event of the CEA is our annual conference which has attracted a great deal of interests from professional economists in higher education, business, and government sector. In addition, we have successfully obtained 6 years' ESRC grant used to organise seminars on Chinese economy which cover the main fields of present-day debate.
The successful development of the CEA would not have been possible without generous financial assistance from many institutions and organisations. We must pay warm tributes to all of our sponsors. For this years' conference, we would like to express our sincere thanks to the Department for International Development, the Education Section of the Chinese Embassy, the Middlesex University and many other private and public organisations and corporations.
I like to use this occasion to express our extreme sadness on the death of our former trustee, Sir Alec Cairncross. He had been highly supportive for the CEA and its activities. On the other hand, I am delighted to announce that Professor Charles Goodhart from the London School of Economics has accepted our invitation to become the new trustee of the CEA. I trust that the CEA will benefit greatly from Professor Goodhartâ€™s expertise and his willingness to serve.
I am particularly pleased today that Ambassador Ma Zhenggang is here to open this important conference despite his busy schedule. The theme of this conference is about Chinese Economy and Industry in the next millennium. China's spectacular economic growth over the last 20 years is universally acknowledged. However, there is still substantial disagreement within the economics profession on factors contributing to Chinaâ€™s mysterious performance. A full appreciation of the Chinese experience in recent years has important policy implications, not only for China herself, but also for other developing countries and economies in transition.
Our discussion will be initiated by a number of invited speakers. Our distinguished speakers today will include Dr Cyril Lin, University of Oxford, Professor Peter Nolan, University of Cambridge, Professor Charles Goodhart, London School of Economics (our new trustee), Professor Haiwen, Beijing University, Professor John Wong, National University of Singapore, and Professor Howard Davies, Hong Kong Polytechnic University.
After the general sessions today, we will have three parallel sessions tomorrow. These parallel sessions will focus on more specialised areas of interests, including Financial Market Reforms; Economic Growth; and Regional Development.
As I am sure that you are waiting impatiently to hear what our speakers have to say, I shall not extend my introduction any further. But, before I invite Ambassador Ma to give an opening address, as President of the CEA, may I thank you all once again for gracing the conference by your presence.
